## Miracles and Modern Objections

When a large part of humanity began to focus on understanding the material world and using it for its own benefit, many new objections to religion appeared with great confidence. As a result, some Muslims found it difficult to answer them, and some sincere Muslims, unable to respond, made extreme compromises concerning the very foundations of religion. The main reason was that Muslims were unfamiliar with the nature of these modern fields of knowledge and lacked confidence, while those presenting scientific knowledge were highly confident in their own learning. Although these doubts and objections were answered effectively in later periods, a group among us is still living with them.

Falsehood does not remain in one form. It comes in different disguises and uses different means. For this reason, it seemed necessary to answer the old questions that are now reaching the younger generation through social media in entirely new forms.

The question, then, is this: Are miracles compatible with science?

To answer it, we first need to understand fully the nature of miracles, karāmāt, and science.

## The Nature of Miracles and Karāmāt

The accounts of the prophets' miracles in the Qur'an can, in a broad sense, be divided into two categories.

The first category consists of miracles presented when a messenger claimed prophethood. This was an enormous claim. When a person says, "I am a representative of the Lord of the worlds, and I am going to convey His commands," the question naturally arises: What evidence supports the messenger's claim? The messenger's character, moral conduct, sound disposition, and the message he conveyed were themselves evidence. Yet not every mind was satisfied by that evidence. Therefore, an extraordinary event, impossible according to the known laws of nature, would occur through the messenger. But the decision about whether such an extraordinary event was needed belonged to the Lord of the worlds Himself. Allah was the true agent of that event.

For example, the Qur'an mentions such events in connection with Jesus, Salih, and Moses, peace be upon them. It does not mention such an event in the cases of Noah, Hud, and Lot, peace be upon them. Another point must be understood here. These extraordinary events were not presented to prove God's existence. Primarily, they served as evidence that the messenger was a messenger, and secondarily, as evidence that his teachings were true. It should also be clear that no prophet claimed he could perform an extraordinary act whenever he wished.

The second category consists of miracles that occurred to help and support a prophet by Allah's command. Such help includes teaching the prophet, placing reassurance and tranquility in his heart, protecting him from the hostility of his enemies, or meeting a particular need through an extraordinary means. The Miʿraj, and the support given at Badr and in the Cave of Thawr, are examples. These miracles, too, do not depend on a person's will. No one can decide to demonstrate such an event whenever he wishes, in a public place or in a laboratory.

Some miracles belong to both categories, such as the fire becoming cool for Abraham, peace be upon him.

Karāmāt are extraordinary events that occur through someone who is not a prophet. Since a non-prophet has no need for an extraordinary event of the first category, only the second category remains in the form of karāmāt.

## What Science Is — and Its Limits

Modern science is a branch of knowledge based on observation and experiment. For something to count as science, knowledge must be grounded in observation and experiment. A single observation or experiment is not enough. It must also be repeated as needed. In addition, it must be possible to make predictions based on the conclusions drawn from those observations and experiments.

To make research easier, science also establishes certain basic principles. For example, it holds that matter and energy can neither be created nor destroyed. The matter and energy in this universe continually change their forms. Science also holds that the universe is a closed system. In other words, it claims that whatever happens within the universe has its cause within the universe. These two points — the confinement of matter and energy to the universe, and the universe being a closed system — are basic assumptions on which the entire structure of science rests.

These two principles are fundamental to scientific research. Scientific progress could not have advanced so far if these principles had not been established in advance. If people attributed every event to an intentional being outside the universe, it would be extremely difficult to discover the rules and laws by which the universe operates. Instead of discovering a law, people would turn their attention to finding out what that external power intended.

As explained above, experiment and observation are fundamental to scientific research. A large part of science therefore rests on inductive reasoning. Scientists repeat different experiments to reach a conclusion. When the experiments support that conclusion, they confirm it on the assumption that the same experiment will always necessarily produce the same result.

The problem with this method of research is that limited experiments are used to draw unlimited conclusions.

A second problem with this method is that a result is accepted on the basis of experiment alone, without knowing why it occurred.

For example, Newton observed that apples always fall down from trees, that it takes more effort to climb to a greater height, and that planets orbit the sun without leaving their paths, even though objects generally travel in straight lines. He concluded that matter attracts other matter according to its mass. This property of attraction is called gravity. On this basis, we can use mathematical formulas to predict how a body will move. But this research does not tell us why matter attracts other matter.

Many matters are closely connected with our consciousness, yet we cannot explain them within the scientific fields described above. For example, has the universe always existed, or did it begin at some point in time? Does the universe have a creator? What is the nature of human consciousness? Is it simply part of our biological brain, or is it something beyond the brain? In matters like these, we use reason to reflect and try to draw conclusions through deductive reasoning. We do so because our observation and experience are very limited. This is called rational inquiry.

One of the central debates in philosophy concerns the source of knowledge. Does knowledge come from experience, or from rational inquiry? We will not enter into that debate here. The purpose of mentioning it is to make clear that scientific knowledge has limits. Science, in itself, does not encompass every field of knowledge.

Because science rests on certain basic assumptions, we can never say with certainty that the knowledge gained from a scientific discovery is final.

Because science draws unlimited conclusions from limited experiments, we can never be certain that the result will remain as we expect if there is a fundamental change in the conditions or parameters involved.

Because science cannot explain the cause of a result, we can never be sure that something science considers final today will still be considered final tomorrow. When science explains the cause of a phenomenon, another "why" lies within that explanation. When that new "why" is answered, there is always another "why" behind it.

## Science Is Never Final

Consider an example of scientific uncertainty.

As mentioned above, Newton discovered gravity — Newton's law of gravitation — on the basis of his experiments. He also formulated the three famous laws of motion, known as Newton's laws of motion. These discoveries are why Newton is regarded as one of the greatest scientists in the world.

But as technology advanced, new methods of making more precise measurements were developed. It became clear that the results derived from Newton's laws contained errors that could be seen with these new methods. In the early twentieth century, Einstein proposed two theories called the Special Theory of Relativity and the General Theory of Relativity. According to the General Theory of Relativity, wherever there is matter, space bends towards it. A body may appear to be moving straight through space, but because space itself has bent, its path curves towards the matter.

Research into motion also showed that Newton's formula fails when a body moves at a very high speed. Einstein's research showed that the closer a body's speed gets to the speed of light, the greater the error in Newton's formula. To address this, Einstein developed a new theory called the Special Theory of Relativity. According to this theory, distance and time contract based on the speed of a body. One consequence of the theory is that time is also a dimension of space. Just as length, width, and height are dimensions of space, time is also part of space.

Another consequence of this theory is that matter and energy are two different forms of the same thing. Matter can be converted into energy, and energy into matter. Nuclear research was carried out on the basis of this theory, and uranium was partially converted into energy. This is used in atomic bombs and nuclear reactors.

These two theories brought about a major revolution in the scientific world. They enabled people to see existence, time, space, and matter in an entirely different way.

Before going further, it is also worth knowing that during Einstein's own era, a theory emerged that went beyond the Theory of Relativity. It is called Quantum Theory. It untangled some problems but created further questions. Quantum physics was new research into the fundamental particles of matter and energy. Until then, science held that everything in the universe follows a fixed set of rules, and that if we knew those rules, we could predict the movement or behavior of every particle with complete accuracy. But quantum physics showed that, at the level of the most fundamental particles, matter behaves randomly and unpredictably. The average or overall behavior and movement of these particles can, however, be determined.

There is a contradiction between Einstein's General Theory of Relativity and Quantum Theory, and no way has yet been found to resolve it. To address this contradiction, scientists have proposed accepting additional dimensions beyond the four dimensions of space, dimensions that human consciousness cannot perceive.

The example of gravity given above makes several things clear.

Objects falling down is an ordinary observation familiar to everyone. When people became curious about objects and the laws of nature, they developed the theory of gravity on the basis of the knowledge available at the time. It was accepted for almost three centuries.

But when new knowledge and measuring instruments became available, the errors in a theory that had been accepted for three centuries became clear.

A new theory was then developed. Its mathematical calculations are entirely correct, but it does not agree with the theory of quantum physics. To reconcile these two theories, it has been proposed that space has more than four dimensions.

We are therefore entirely justified in concluding that no scientific research is final. As long as new discoveries and information continue to emerge, science will keep changing its theories. We will never be in a position to claim that science has discovered the complete reality of any law.

There are contradictions and gaps in scientific discoveries, and scientists continue trying to reconcile those contradictions.

The astonishing discoveries of the present age have no parallel in human history. Deeply impressed by scientific progress, some people who revere science conclude that rational inquiry is now unnecessary and that the scientific method can meet every intellectual need. The truth is that, despite these astonishing discoveries, it is plain that each new scientific discovery opens one layer of the laws of nature, beneath which lies another, even thicker layer. Trying to remove rational inquiry from human knowledge and put science in its place is therefore futile. No matter how much science has advanced, it is far too soon to claim that it can replace rational inquiry. Science will remain within its own limits. There is no doubt that scientific progress has greatly affected our way of life and our way of thinking. But the claim that science can discover the complete reality of anything is baseless. Science is a tool for understanding, and there is nothing wrong with using it.

## The Scientific Explanation of Miracles

Now let us turn to the scientific explanation of miracles. There are two possible positions on this matter.

The first is that all miracles occurred in accordance with the laws of nature. But because we do not yet know the laws of nature completely, and do not claim that we do, we are unable to reconcile these miracles with the known laws of nature. It should be clear that a miracle's occurring in accordance with the laws of nature does not affect its essential status as a miracle. For example, suppose someone proves in the future that there is a scientific explanation for the path that opened through the sea for Moses, peace be upon him. Even then, the explanation for those paths opening, and for Moses and his people arriving at the sea at precisely that time, can only be the will of Allah. This striking coincidence is ultimately a matter of divine will, not science.

The second position is that, although every particle in the universe operates according to fixed scientific laws by Allah's command, that does not mean Allah's creatures cannot depart from those laws by His command. The belief that all creatures always follow the laws of nature and never depart from them is an assumption of science, without which science cannot take even one step forward. But the claim that this assumption is a universal and self-evident truth is an utterly absurd claim.

There is therefore absolutely no need to provide a scientific explanation for miracles. When science becomes capable of claiming that it has discovered every reality in the universe, then let it come forward. We can discuss the matter then.

It has already become clear that, in both categories of miracles, the person through whom a miracle occurs cannot claim that he can repeat it whenever he wishes. Miracles therefore do not fall within the scope of experimental science.

In this context, it becomes clear that those who object to miracles on the basis of science insist on two basic errors.

First, they treat scientific discoveries as final. Second, they assume that all fields of knowledge fall within the scope of the natural sciences.

If we accept the first position described above, it remains unproven that science and miracles contradict each other. If we accept the second position, there is no need at all to reconcile miracles with science. An internal contradiction within something is one matter. Something's not conforming to science is another.

## Belief in Miracles and Faith in the Unseen

At this point, a question arises. If a miracle cannot be repeated through experiment, what does it mean to believe in it?

First, it should be clear that belief in a miracle is not a basic part of Islam's invitation. For those who witnessed a miracle, it was evidence of a prophet's prophethood. But after the Seal of the Prophets departed this world, we do not invite people to accept him as a prophet because he performed the miracle of splitting the moon, or because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, made the journey of the Miʿraj. Today, belief in miracles is a matter of faith, not evidence of prophethood.

We believe in miracles because Allah has power over all things. The laws of nature that science strives to discover were made by Allah, and He can change them whenever He wills. It should be clear here that some people call scientific laws the Sunnah of Allah and claim that Allah's Sunnah can never change. In the Qur'an, the Sunnah of Allah refers to something else, so there should be no confusion about this. The Sunnah of Allah is whatever Allah Himself describes as His Sunnah.

Today, we invite people to Islam on the basis of the Qur'an, which is itself a living miracle of the Prophet and can still be witnessed. The Qur'an appeals to the human faculties of reason, aesthetic appreciation, moral judgment, and eloquence. Those who demand miracles perceptible to the senses as a condition for guidance are, in reality, rejecting faith in the unseen. In other words, they demand that what they are asked to believe must be witnessed directly, even when there is rational evidence for it. At the very beginning of the Qur'an, it is made clear that faith in the unseen is a condition for guidance. One principle concerning witnessing a miracle is that, after it has been witnessed, the period of reprieve comes to an end. It has therefore been Allah's wisdom that people should now come to faith on the basis of their understanding, their aesthetic sense, and their moral judgment.

## Conclusions

Several conclusions follow from our discussion.

1. In our time, the basis of our invitation is not miracles perceived by the senses, but an appeal to human understanding. Today, the reality of miracles is a matter of belief, not the basis of our invitation. Miracles were only meant to lift the veil of the unseen temporarily. Faith in the unseen remains a condition for guidance.

2. The fact that something has not been observed or tested does not prove that it is contrary to reason. Many branches of knowledge rely entirely on rational inquiry.

3. From a scientific point of view, it is incorrect to claim that science has become familiar with every aspect of reality. It is therefore not necessary for a genuine miracle to conform to known scientific principles. Present scientific knowledge contains contradictions within itself.

4. Science is only one branch of human knowledge. Some aspects of human knowledge lie beyond the scope of science. A scientific explanation of miracles is therefore unnecessary.

5. According to Islamic belief, no human being can repeat a miracle or karāmah by experiment and demonstrate it at will. Trying to prove a miracle through scientific experiment is therefore futile. When no such claim is being made, demanding proof is meaningless.
