This article examines an argument associated with hadith deniers, particularly the Submitters movement linked to Rashad Khalifa https://qurantalk.gitbook.io/shahada . The argument maintains that the declaration of faith consists only of “There is no god but Allah” (Lā ilāha illā Allāh) and that the testimony to Muhammad’s messengership is not part of it. It is based on an examination of approximately fifty verses in which the statement of divine oneness appears without an immediately connected mention of messengership.

The argument is evaluated below in light of its methodology, Qur’anic texts, context, and the practical mass transmission of the Muslim community. Since the proponent does not accept hadith as authoritative, the main discussion is based on the Qur’an. The Sunnah is presented at the end as supporting evidence, particularly in view of its continuous practice across the Muslim community.

Arabic passages are paired with the English quotations below. […] marks omitted words from a verse.

## First: A Methodological Flaw—Inferring Nonexistence from Silence

The entire argument rests on the premise that because the verses mentioning divine oneness do not mention messengership, messengership is not part of the declaration of faith. This reasoning has two fundamental flaws:

The absence of a mention does not establish the absence of a ruling. The fact that a text does not mention something does not necessarily mean that it denies it, unless the wording indicates exclusivity. In these verses, exclusivity concerns divinity—that there is no deity besides Allah—not the components of the declaration of faith.

The examination is incomplete. The proponent has collected only the verses that appear to support the argument and omitted the texts that make belief in the Messenger integral to belief in Allah. The established principle that “the Qur’an explains part of itself through other parts” requires that a ruling on any subject be based on all relevant texts, rather than on a selected portion of them.

## Second: The Proponent’s Own Citation of Al-A‘raf 7:158 Refutes the Argument

The proponent includes Surah Al-A‘raf, verse 158, in the list of evidence, even though this verse places divine oneness and messengership within the same passage:

قُلْ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنِّي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكُمْ جَمِيعًا الَّذِي لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ فَآمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ النَّبِيِّ الْأُمِّيِّ […] وَاتَّبِعُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَهْتَدُونَ

> “Say, ‘O humanity, I am truly the Messenger of Allah to you all. To Him belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth. There is no god but Him. He gives life and causes death. So believe in Allah and His Messenger, the unlettered Prophet … and follow him so that you may be guided.’”

The verse begins with the declaration of messengership: “I am truly the Messenger of Allah to you all.” The statement of divine oneness follows, then comes the command, “So believe in Allah and His Messenger,” and finally, guidance is connected to following the Messenger. Thus, the very text cited in support of the argument contradicts its claim.

## Third: Explicit Qur’anic Texts on Testifying to Muhammad’s Messengership

### 1. The second part of the declaration appears verbatim in the Qur’an

مُحَمَّدٌ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ […]

> “Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.” (Al-Fath 48:29)

### 2. The Qur’an explicitly mentions the testimony to messengership

إِذَا جَاءَكَ الْمُنَافِقُونَ قَالُوا نَشْهَدُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُ اللَّهِ وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ إِنَّكَ لَرَسُولُهُ وَاللَّهُ يَشْهَدُ إِنَّ الْمُنَافِقِينَ لَكَاذِبُونَ

> “When the hypocrites come to you, they say, ‘We bear witness that you are indeed the Messenger of Allah.’ Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars.” (Al-Munafiqun 63:1)

This verse establishes three points:

During the Prophet’s lifetime, testifying to his messengership was a recognized expression of Islam. The hypocrites used these words to present themselves as Muslims.

Allah confirms the content of the testimony by saying, “Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger.”

The hypocrites are described as liars because of what they concealed in their hearts, not because the words of the testimony were incorrect.

If testimony to the Messenger’s status were not part of faith, expressing it would not have served as an indication of a claim to faith.

### 3. Texts that require belief in Allah and His Messenger together

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا آمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَالْكِتَابِ الَّذِي نَزَّلَ عَلَىٰ رَسُولِهِ […]

> “O you who believe, believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Book He has revealed to His Messenger …” (Al-Nisa’ 4:136)

لِتُؤْمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ […]

> “… so that you may believe in Allah and His Messenger …” (Al-Fath 48:9)

فَآمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَالنُّورِ الَّذِي أَنْزَلْنَا […]

> “So believe in Allah and His Messenger and the Light We have sent down.” (Al-Taghabun 64:8)

إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ […]

> “The believers are only those who have believed in Allah and His Messenger …” (Al-Hujurat 49:15; see also Al-Nur 24:62)

The word innamā (“only”) expresses restriction. These verses define believers through the combined requirements of belief in Allah and belief in His Messenger.

## Fourth: The Proper Context of the Main Verses Cited by the Proponent

### 1. The covenant verse (Al-A‘raf 7:172)

[…] أَلَسْتُ بِرَبِّكُمْ قَالُوا بَلَىٰ شَهِدْنَا […]

> “Am I not your Lord?” They said, “Yes indeed; we bear witness.”

This is the innate covenant concerning the Lordship of Allah, taken from all the children of Adam. It is not a statement defining the Islamic declaration of faith for the community of Muhammad.

A separate covenant was also taken from the Prophets: that they would believe in and support the Messenger who would come after them:

[…] لَتُؤْمِنُنَّ بِهِ وَلَتَنْصُرُنَّهُ […]

> “You must believe in him and support him.” (Al ‘Imran 3:81)

This shows that the Qur’an treats belief in the Messenger as a distinct covenant.

### 2. Al ‘Imran 3:18

شَهِدَ اللَّهُ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ […]

> “Allah bears witness that there is no god but Him …”

The subject of this verse is Allah’s own testimony to His oneness. It does not set out the components of the declaration of faith made by human beings. The absence of a mention of messengership is entirely consistent with the subject of the passage, which is divinity.

### 3. Muhammad 47:19

فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَاسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنْبِكَ […]

> “Know, then, that there is no god but Allah, and seek forgiveness for your sin …”

This is a command concerning knowledge and conviction in divine oneness and the seeking of forgiveness. It is not a definition of the components of the declaration of faith. In the same surah, verse 2 describes valid belief as follows:

وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَآمَنُوا بِمَا نُزِّلَ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَهُوَ الْحَقُّ مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ […]

> “As for those who believe and do good deeds and believe in what has been revealed to Muhammad—and it is the truth from their Lord …” (Muhammad 47:2)

## Fifth: Using the Statements of Earlier Prophets as Evidence—A False Comparison

The proponent cites the statements of Prophets Noah, Hud, Salih, and Shu‘ayb, peace be upon them:

[…] يَا قَوْمِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مَا لَكُمْ مِنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرُهُ […]

> “O my people, worship Allah; you have no god other than Him.”

(Al-A‘raf 7:59, 7:65, 7:73, 7:85; Hud 11:50, 11:61, 11:84)

However, the Qur’an repeatedly mentions the other part of their message as well:

إِنِّي لَكُمْ رَسُولٌ أَمِينٌ ۝ فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ

> “I am truly a trustworthy messenger to you. So be mindful of Allah and obey me.”

(Al-Shu‘ara’ 26:107–108, 26:125–126, 26:143–144, 26:162–163, 26:178–179)

This shows that every Prophet’s message included, alongside the oneness of Allah, recognition of his own messengership and obedience to him. It is self-evident that earlier communities were not addressed with the words “Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah,” because they were addressed by their own respective Prophets. This establishes that in every era, belief in the Messenger of that era was required alongside belief in the oneness of Allah. After the coming of the Seal of the Prophets, this testimony took the specific form: “Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.”

## Sixth: The Qur’anic Ruling on Separating Allah from His Messengers

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَكْفُرُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَيُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يُفَرِّقُوا بَيْنَ اللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَيَقُولُونَ نُؤْمِنُ بِبَعْضٍ وَنَكْفُرُ بِبَعْضٍ وَيُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يَتَّخِذُوا بَيْنَ ذَٰلِكَ سَبِيلًا ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْكَافِرُونَ حَقًّا […]

> “Indeed, those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and seek to make a distinction between Allah and His messengers, saying, ‘We believe in some and disbelieve in others,’ and seeking to adopt a way in between—they are truly the disbelievers.” (Al-Nisa’ 4:150–151)

The Qur’an calls the separation of belief in Allah from belief in His messengers “making a distinction” and describes those who do so as “the true disbelievers.” If the declaration of faith depended on belief in divine oneness alone, then any monotheist among the People of the Book would be deemed a believer merely by saying “There is no god but Allah.” This conclusion directly contradicts these verses.

## Seventh: Continuous Practice and the Consensus of the Muslim Community

Even someone who does not accept hadith as authoritative must acknowledge that the words “I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah” are recited many times each day in the call to prayer, the call immediately before prayer, and the sitting recitation in prayer. This practice has been transmitted continuously throughout the Muslim community, in the same manner of practical mass transmission through which the Qur’an itself has been transmitted. Denying this continuous practice would call into question the very basis on which the Qur’an has been transmitted.

Authentic hadith also explicitly support this:

“Islam is built upon five: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah …” (Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 8; Sahih Muslim, no. 16)

In the Hadith of Gabriel: “Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.” (Sahih Muslim, no. 8)

In the tashahhud narrated by Ibn Mas‘ud, which the Hanafi school follows: “I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.” (Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 831)

In Al-‘Aqidah al-Tahawiyyah, Imam al-Tahawi mentions the messengership of Muhammad as part of the creed immediately after discussing divine oneness:

“And Muhammad is His chosen servant, His selected Prophet, and His approved Messenger.”

## Conclusion

The argument examined here is methodologically flawed because it treats the absence of a mention as proof that something is excluded, and because it relies on an incomplete examination of the relevant texts. The verses it cites are concerned, in their context, with the oneness of Allah; they do not define the components of the Islamic declaration of faith.

By contrast, explicit texts—including Al-A‘raf 7:158, Al-Fath 48:29, Al-Munafiqun 63:1, Al-Nisa’ 4:136 and 4:150–151, and Al-Hujurat 49:15—establish testimony to Muhammad’s messengership as an essential part of faith. In light of the Qur’an, the continuously transmitted Sunnah, and the consensus of the Muslim community, the declaration of faith comprises both testimony to divine oneness—“I bear witness that there is no god but Allah”—and testimony to messengership—“I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.” Neither part can be omitted from a valid declaration of faith.
